## 2. The fiction: future hockey as bloodsport

HyperBlade is a mutated hockey/jai-alai sport in a violent future. Gamecenter dates the fiction to **2065** \[U\], and the game's arena screen fits a mid-21st-century setting (a drome "built 2057" with 699 recorded fatalities). Wizbang pitched it as "futuristic in-line hockey-like ... played more like jai-alai in battle armor with a big spiky sword-like mitt and a hurkin' nasty rock", with "no penalty box except the kind you bury and put flowers on" ([Wizbang site, 1996](https://web.archive.org/web/19970130162706/http://www.wizbang.com/hyperblade.htm)).

Play happens on skates inside an **elliptical bowl called a drome**, with one small goal cut into the wall at each end; press descriptions range from "the inside of a zeppelin" to "an elongated half-pipe" and "a drained swimming pool" ([Next Generation #15](https://archive.org/details/nextgen-issue-015); [PC Gamer](https://web.archive.org/web/19991209061404/http://www.pcgamer.com/reviews/547.html); [Gamecenter](https://archive.kontek.net/gotcha.classicgaming.gamespy.com/gamecenter/HyperBlade%20-%20PC%20Review%20-%20Gamecenter%20-%20CNET_com.htm)). Walls are skateable: players ride the curve, catch air, flip and 360.

Equipment (manual unless noted): the **rok**, a loose ball/"rock" that is carried, passed and thrown; the **jak**, a bladed glove used to pick up the rok, mines and swarfs and to slash ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HyperBlade); [GameSpot](https://www.gamespot.com/reviews/hyperblade-review/1900-2537770/)); the **killball**, a rok electrified by the Killball Charger into an auto-seeking projectile; the **swarf**, a homing stun projectile; and **mines**. Wikipedia's claims that steroids/bionics are legal and decapitation is survivable are unsourced \[U\].

## 3. Rules, scoring and match flow

**Object.** Score more than the opponent over three periods by putting the rok in their goal, carried or thrown. There are no referees and no penalties \[V\].

- **Periods.** Three periods. Wikipedia says length is selectable at 3/5/7 minutes \[U\]; a preview mentions a "15-minute game", which fits a 5-minute default ([Next Generation #15](https://archive.org/details/nextgen-issue-015)).
- **Scoring.** Base goal value is multiplied **×2–4** by the Multiplier object \[V\], which explains the double-figure scores. Next Generation expected average scores "in the 20s".
- **Health and death.** Hits drain a skater's health bar; at zero he is killed or injured, removed from the drome, and his slot stays empty until the next goal or period end \[V — manual + [Old-Games/Games Domain](https://www.old-games.com/download/4692/hyperblade)\]. Healthy players can be substituted "on the fly" with keys 1/2; each side has two reserves \[V\]. In gauntlet mode dead athletes are lost for the tournament \[V — Games Domain\].
- **Goalie.** Computer-controlled and "cannot be killed" ([Old-Games](https://www.old-games.com/download/4692/hyperblade)); Wikipedia adds "invulnerable except mines" \[U\]. He attacks skaters who bring the rok too close \[V — manual tip\].
- **Annihilation.** Killing all four of the opposing team's forwards and then scoring wins outright, regardless of score ([Games Domain text](https://www.myabandonware.com/game/hyperblade-dgd)) \[U — one detailed secondary source\]. Wikipedia's "an annihilated team can still win on points" contradicts it; edge cases unresolved.
- **Awards.** Wikipedia lists **Supernova** (30 points in a game), **Anvil** (eliminate a team for the season) and **Archer** (all shots converted) \[U\]; the shipped UI has an **AWARDS** screen.
- **Modes.** Training (default), **Exhibition** (choose both teams and drome) and **Gauntlet** (beat all eleven opposing teams without a loss) \[V — manual + Games Domain\]. The March 1996 preview describes a bigger planned structure — 16-game season with play-offs, six-game tournament, single-game and play-offs-only modes, hidden teams/arenas \[U — pre-release plan\].

## 4. Teams, rosters and players

- **12 teams, 12 dromes, 45 unique players**, three skill levels ([MobyGames](https://www.mobygames.com/game/1402/hyperblade/); [Old-Games](https://www.old-games.com/download/4692/hyperblade)) \[U counts, repeated across sources\]. Preview: six US/Canada teams plus one each from the UK, Japan, Germany, Russia and Poland \[U\].
- **Named teams:** Los Angeles Shockwave, Chicago Machine, Tokyo Ronins, Moscow T-34s ([PC Gamer](https://web.archive.org/web/19991209061404/http://www.pcgamer.com/reviews/547.html)); a Seattle side shown as "The Fury" in the HUD and a slow Leeds side ([Computer Games Magazine](https://web.archive.org/web/20030523220506/http://www.cdmag.com/articles/008/022/hyperblade_review.html); press screenshots). The full twelve-team roster was not found \[U\].
- **Squad structure:** two forwards + two reserves + one goalie; two outfield skaters on the ice per side. Gamecenter calls the outfield roles "striker" and "defender"; other sources say "two forwards" \[U on labels, V on 2+2+1\].
- **Players differ** in strength, speed, stamina and "rok handling", and teams have distinct styles and AI routines (Leeds stalls; Chicago brutalises) \[V — PC Gamer, CDMag, old-games\]. The start-up screen lets you pick the starting forwards \[V\].
- **Roster HUD:** yellow = you, red = other players on the ice, green = bench (quick reference) \[V\].

## 5. Controls and combat

Keyboard-first; PC Gamer recommended a gamepad, and complained about unintuitive controls. The manual's key diagram and quick-reference scans are partly illegible, so the exact key map is reconstructed \[U\]; the semantic bindings are solid.

**Documented:** **S** is the Attack key (slash, plant mine, launch swarf); **S + arrows** gives special fighting moves in Expert Attack; **1/2** substitute; **spacebar** shows the Shot Cam when carrying the rok; **Esc** pauses; **F7/F8** toggle bilinear filtering/drome textures; keypad resizes the screen. One context key is **Pass** on offence and **Change Player** on defence; there are **Speedburst**, **Jump**, **Power Shot** and **Call for Pass** bindings (quick reference) \[V\]. Beginners use **Auto Attack + Quick Shot**; advanced players switch to **Expert Attack** \[V\].

| Move | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| Body check | Knocks down, can dislodge the rok \[V\] |
| Slash (jak) | Core damage; repeated slashing can **decapitate** \[V\] |
| Punch / kick / trip / side punch | Strikes and spills; side punch is a double-tap left/right variant \[U — manual/reviews\] |
| Jump / wall ride / flip / 360 / rail grind | Traversal and evasive tricks; preview says tricks can buff stats until injury \[V/U\] |
| Speedburst | Short acceleration; HUD indicator implies a resource \[V\] |
| Power Shot / Quick Shot | Shot types when carrying the rok; GameSpot notes climactic power shots \[V/U\] |
| Killball throw | Auto-seeking electrocuting projectile after the charger \[V\] |
| Mine / Swarf | Plant / launch with Attack \[V\] |
| Decapitation | Attested; Wikipedia's "head becomes the new rok" is unconfirmed by the manual \[U\] |

## 6. Drome object catalogue (primary source)

The quick-reference card defines these; each drome runs a home/visitor object set shown on the arena screen \[V\].

| Object | Behaviour and counter |
| --- | --- |
| **Killball Charger** | Carry the rok through to convert it; point at an opponent and press Attack — the killball seeks |
| **Turnstile** | Skate through to spin its razor arms at pursuers; avoid the centre pole |
| **Multiplier** | ×2–4 next goal; may knock you around but does no health damage |
| **Hurdle** | Concrete barrier that disorients; jump or turn, and bait pursuers into it |
| **Laser Hurdle** | Waist-height lethal laser; jump it, never skate through |
| **Randomizer** | Lip flags grant random power-ups: opponent slow-down, health, long shot, immunity, more |
| **Ramp** | Launches you airborne to gain ground or dodge objects/players |
| **Armory** | Pick up mines and swarfs, first come first served |
| **Mine** | Proximity explosive, planted with Attack; seed chokepoints and your own goalmouth |
| **Swarf** | Homing anti-personnel projectile that stuns; good for jarring the rok loose |

## 7. Presentation and HUD

Per the quick-reference card \[V\]: **shot power meter**; **Shot Cam** (spacebar); **Rear-View Cam** that appears when an opponent closes from behind; **timer/period indicator**; **speedburst indicator**; **roster** (yellow you / red others playing / green bench); **radar** (rok yellow, teammate blue, opponents bright and dark red); **rok finder** (white direction line); **health bar** (controlled player yellow, others in shades of red); **goal finder** (radar-rim pointer when carrying).

Press screenshots corroborate: segmented health bar (10–90) and roster column top-left; period clock with three pips top-centre; labelled "Rear View" inset top-right; two-row **LOS/SEA** score strip lower-left; circular radar lower-right; bottom-centre announcement banner (e.g. "The Shockwave Score!"). Public press captures are mirrored in the gitignored `reference/` folder.

**Camera:** a tight chase/over-the-shoulder view; PC Gamer found the rapid arena swivel disorienting. A preview describes the default as "second-person over-the-shoulder" with an optional moving third-person camera for two-player co-op \[U — terminology varies\].

**Menus/UI:** rendered as 3D spaces — lobby, a "PLAYERS ONLY" tunnel to the locker room, and a **Holoviewer** for options and multiplayer. The arena-information screen is strong fiction reference: team banner (**chicago machine**), comment, capacity, year built, "restraint system", fatality count, sponsor, home/visitor object legend, rosters, statistics, arena, game objects, and an AWARDS button. Chicago's entry: capacity 120,000; built 2057; restraint system "Hi-Frequency Noise"; fatalities 699; sponsor "BioBriefs"; comment that its "Machine Shop" leads the league in arena casualties. Intros show "Welcome to HOUSTON", "EXHIBITION MATCH", team logos and "click to continue" \[V — screenshots\].

**Visuals/audio:** flat-shaded polygons for players and arenas — a deliberate trade-off. Producer Lewis Peterson: a texture-mapped polygon cost "about 10 times the processor budget" of a flat-shaded one \[V — [Next Generation #15](https://archive.org/details/nextgen-issue-015), quoted by [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HyperBlade)\]. Optional Direct3D texture-mapping and bilinear filtering shipped; 3D-card owners got textured players and drome surfaces \[V — manual\]. Motion capture came from top inline skaters; music is an original score by **Jeehun Hwang**, with credited sound design and player voices \[V — [MobyGames credits archive](https://web.archive.org/web/20150513092417/http://www.mobygames.com/game/windows/hyperblade/credits)\]. Crowd noise, sirens and explosions are repeatedly praised; blood can be toggled off (Games Domain) \[U\].

## 8. Multiplayer

LAN only: **2–4 players**, IPX/SPX or TCP/IP via DirectPlay. The host picks teams, drome, period length and slots; unselected forwards are AI and goalies stay AI. A chat box sits in the lobby. Modem and Internet play are explicitly unsupported in the shipped manual \[V\]. A pre-release preview promised modem head-to-head and mentioned a possible six-player mode; neither shipped \[U\].

## 9. Development and technology

Wizbang! Software Productions was founded in **1994**; HyperBlade was its first product. It was built on Wizbang's **ADLIB** behaviour-authoring language and, by the studio's claim, was one of the first games to genuinely use the Windows 95 DirectX SDK \[V — [Wizbang "Wiz-Who"](https://web.archive.org/web/19970130162659/http://www.wizbang.com/whizwho.htm)\]. Announced as *Hockeydrome* for PlayStation, Saturn and 32X, it shipped only on Windows 95 \[U — Wikipedia citing 1995–96 magazines\]. It made the cover of Next Generation's March 1996 issue (preview on pp. 46–48) \[V — [scan](https://archive.org/details/nextgen-issue-015)\]. Key credits: Lewis S. Peterson (director/producer), Stephen Morris (lead design), Mike Adams (AI), George Sutty (networking), Wyndham Chow (art), Jeehun Hwang (music) \[V — MobyGames credits\]. Graphics-card vendors bundled it as a Direct3D showcase \[U — Wikipedia\].

## 10. Reception and legacy

Scores: **PC Gamer 78%** (Feb 1997, Todd Vaughn), **Computer Games Magazine 3/5** (1997, Steve Bauman), Coming Soon 86, Gamesmania 80, Gamezilla 94, Game.EXE 70, PC Player Germany 60, PC Games Germany 53, High Score 2/5; **MobyRank 68**. [GameSpot's review](https://www.gamespot.com/reviews/hyperblade-review/1900-2537770/) (5 Dec 1996, Tim Soete) is enthusiastic but the archived page exposes no numeric score.

Recurring praise: motion-captured skating, spectacle, flat-shaded look, 3D-accelerator support, atmosphere. Recurring criticism: disorienting camera and odd controls, cheap/erratic AI, too easy once solved, bugs (3D hardware detection, lock-ups, disappearing roks, substitution glitches), shallow long-term structure. The "3D Speedball" comparison is near-universal: [Gamecenter](https://archive.kontek.net/gotcha.classicgaming.gamespy.com/gamecenter/HyperBlade%20-%20PC%20Review%20-%20Gamecenter%20-%20CNET_com.htm) called it the best future-sport game in six years; [Computer Games Magazine](https://web.archive.org/web/20030523220506/http://www.cdmag.com/articles/008/022/hyperblade_review.html) said it "makes you long for next year's model". Its legacy is as an early DirectX/Direct3D showcase and accelerator-bundle title, a cult violent sports curio with no shipped sequel or console port; later Windows versions are known to run poorly ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HyperBlade)). A demo survives on [archive.org](https://archive.org/details/Hyper_201307); the retail game is preserved by abandonware sites ([My Abandonware](https://www.myabandonware.com/game/hyperblade-dgd)).

## 11. What UltraBlade intentionally changes ([GAME-DESIGN.md](GAME-DESIGN.md))

| Area | HyperBlade (1996) | UltraBlade slice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fiction | 2065 \[U\], real city teams, corporate league | 2099, invented "The Drome League", Blue **ICE SAINTS** vs Red **REDLINE SYNDICATE** |
| Arena | 12 city dromes, per-home object sets, half-pipe/zeppelin | One **"THE VAULT"**, 62 × 34 m black-glass ellipse |
| Match | 3 periods, length selectable (3/5/7) \[U\] | **3 × 90 s**, clock stops after goals |
| Roster | 2 forwards + 2 reserves + CPU goalie; 45 players | 2 active skaters + invulnerable CPU goalie per side; four AI outfield profiles |
| Scoring | Base × Multiplier (2–4×) | **1 point**; multiplier dropped |
| Annihilation | Kill all four forwards, then score | Both enemy skaters down + score ends match (compressed) |
| Death | Permanent; decapitation | Downed until next goal/period; reserve replaces; no dismemberment; gore toggle |
| Moves | Auto/expert attack, body check, trip, slash, punch/kick, speedburst, jump, power/quick shot, pass | Mouse-aimed slash (10), heavy slash (24 + knockdown), body check, boost, jump, pass, charged shot, killball |
| Hazards | Turnstile, laser hurdle, hurdle, ramp, mine, swarf, armory, randomizer, multiplier, charger | Turnstiles, laser hurdle strips, boost strips, killball charger |
| Modes | Training, exhibition, gauntlet, planned season | Single match vs AI; 45 s sudden-death overtime; no season |
| Multiplayer | 2–4 LAN | Out of scope |
| Camera/HUD | Chase cam, rear-view mirror, shot cam, radar, finders | Chase cam + FOV kick, mouse orbit, reticle; scoreboard, clock, roster pips, health/stamina, minimap, kill feed |
| Presentation | Flat-shaded, 640×480/256 colours, optional textures, 3D menus | Neon obsidian arena, bloom, holographic crowd, broadcast overlays, in-world title screen |
| Controls | Keyboard-first, gamepad recommended | WASD + mouse ([CONTROLS.md](CONTROLS.md)) |
| Audio | Original score by Jeehun Hwang | Generated synthwave/SFX, no licensed music |

**Deliberately faithful:** rok/jak/killball vocabulary; charger conversion; turnstiles and laser hurdles; boost; body checks and slashing as risk-reward; health-driven removal with reserve rotation; no referees/penalties; skateable curved boards; in-world menu fiction; goal horn and crowd theatre.

## 12. Contradictions and open questions

- **Release date:** 1 Nov / 12 Dec / 16 Dec 1996. Use "December 1996"; exact day unresolved.
- **Annihilation:** instant win after all four forwards die and you score, vs Wikipedia's claim an annihilated team can still win on points.
- **Period length:** 3/5/7-minute option is Wikipedia-only; manual/preview support a 15-minute match.
- **Controls:** semantic bindings are reliable; exact keys are partly OCR-lost.
- **Goalie:** "invulnerable except mines" is Wikipedia-only.
- **Camera terms** vary between "third person" and "second person".
- **Team roster:** only five or six of twelve teams and none of the 45 players confirmed; "The Fury"/"The Shockwave" as Seattle/LA nicknames is inferred from the HUD.
- **Swarf spelling:** manual "swarf/swarfs" vs Wikipedia "swarves".
- **Decapitation:** attested; "head becomes the new rok" is Wikipedia-only.
- **Sales:** million-copy claim rests on one Wizbang release quoted by Wikipedia.
- **MobyGames "1st-person" tag** contradicts every screenshot and review — disregard.
